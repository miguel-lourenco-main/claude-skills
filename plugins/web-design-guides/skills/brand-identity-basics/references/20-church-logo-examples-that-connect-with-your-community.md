## Use when
Designing a logo for a mission-driven or community institution where the mark must feel authentic to a specific local/denominational identity, not just look good.

## Rules
- Effective community logos share three traits that reinforce each other: simplicity (clean geometric forms, recognizable at any size), meaningful connection to mission/audience, and versatility (works from mobile icon to building signage). Strategic restraint — 2-3 colors, 1-2 fonts — is what makes the other two achievable.
- For denominational/heritage organizations, an established symbol system (e.g. a specific typeface tied to tradition, or an existing denominational mark) can be a legitimate design foundation — build local personality on top of it rather than discarding it.
- Serif typography reads as tradition/authority; sans-serif reads as modern/accessible — choose based on which the institution is actually signaling.
- For multilingual/multicultural audiences, prioritize symbols over text-heavy marks — symbols cross language barriers that wordmarks cannot.
- Test small: verify legibility at social-profile size (~160x160px) and favicon size (16x16px) before finalizing; simplify further if it fails.
- Before any visual work, resolve identity questions first: who are we, who do we serve, what makes us distinct — mission clarity should precede design concepts, not follow them.

## Patterns (name — when to use — when to avoid)
- Negative-space dual-meaning mark (e.g. letterform hidden in shape) — when the concept reinforces the primary message without requiring explanation — avoid if the cleverness makes the mark harder to read at a glance.
- Adopting a free/established network or denominational logo system — for new or resource-constrained organizations — avoid if it erases all local distinctiveness the community actually wants signaled.

## Anti-patterns
- A mark that only works in full color with no plan for single-color or reversed/dark-background versions.
- Starting from visual concepts before mission/audience is defined.

## Checklist (only if supported)
- Logo tested in full color, single color, and reversed on dark backgrounds.
- Legible at 160x160px and 16x16px.
- Palette limited to 2-3 colors, 1-2 fonts.
