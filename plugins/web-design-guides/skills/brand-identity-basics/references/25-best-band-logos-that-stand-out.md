## Use when
Evaluating whether a symbolic/cultural mark (not just a corporate logo) has the qualities that make it endure — applicable to any brand seeking a mark with subcultural or emotional weight rather than pure corporate polish.

## Rules
- Four criteria separate enduring iconic marks from forgettable ones: instant recognition (achieved through radical simplicity, not complexity), versatility (must work at thumbnail size and billboard scale, in full color and single color), genre/personality expression (the mark should signal identity before anyone experiences the product), and reproducibility (a mark simple enough that fans/users can recreate or share it drives organic spread).
- Six recurring design levers behind enduring marks: symbolic simplicity, cultural appropriation/subversion (deliberately recontextualizing existing imagery), typographic distinction, merchandising scalability, temporal transcendence (ages without feeling dated), and single-color reproducibility.
- A crude or "unpolished" execution can be a deliberate strategy: it signals authenticity/anti-establishment positioning and, practically, is often cheaper and easier to reproduce across formats than a polished mark.
- Minimal line-count constructions (e.g. a mark buildable from as few as 4 lines) enable low-fidelity reproduction (hand-drawn, stenciled, spray-painted) which extends organic reach beyond official channels.

## Patterns (name — when to use — when to avoid)
- Deliberately "unpolished"/hand-drawn mark — when authenticity and cheap reproducibility matter more than corporate polish — avoid for brands whose positioning depends on precision/trust (e.g. finance, healthcare).
- Cultural appropriation/subversion of existing imagery — when the brand's identity is explicitly transgressive or countercultural — avoid generally; carries real legal/ethical risk (one example here "sparked legal disputes") and is not a safe default pattern.
- Single evolving character/mascot as the brand face — when there's no consistent human face to the brand (virtual acts, faceless collectives) — avoid if the character can't survive being reimagined across many contexts while staying recognizable.

## Anti-patterns
- A mark that only reads correctly in full color (fails single-color and reversed reproduction).
- Overly complex marks that lose recognition at small scale.

## Checklist (only if supported)
- Mark is recognizable at both thumbnail and large-format scale.
- Mark survives single-color/monochrome reproduction.
- Mark signals brand personality before any other content is experienced.
