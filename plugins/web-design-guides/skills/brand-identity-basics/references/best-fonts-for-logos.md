## Use when
Choosing or evaluating a typeface for a wordmark/logo, or building a font-personality mapping reference for brand work generally.

## Rules
- A "logo-ready" font must meet three criteria: legible from tiny favicon to large signage, communicates brand personality at a glance, and has licensing that covers commercial use — evaluate any font candidate against all three, not aesthetics alone.
- Three-step selection framework: (1) define brand personality (traditional↔modern, serious↔playful, accessible↔exclusive) (2) match personality to font category — serif = tradition/authority/premium, sans-serif = modern/accessible/innovative, bold display = energy/confidence (3) check practical requirements by testing at four sizes: 8pt (mobile favicon), 16pt (mobile text), 36pt (desktop header), 72pt+ (signage).
- High-contrast serifs (thick/thin strokes, e.g. Bodoni-style) signal luxury/elegance but can lose legibility at small sizes — verify at favicon scale before committing to this category for a mark that must also work small.
- Ultra-popular free fonts (e.g. widely-used geometric sans-serifs) carry adoption risk — you may end up visually indistinguishable from competitors using the same font; compensate with a distinctive mark, custom letter-spacing, or strong color system.
- All-caps-only display fonts need a paired secondary font for body text/supporting copy since they have no lowercase.
- Thin-weight fonts lose legibility at small sizes regardless of category — for logo/favicon use, stay at medium weight or above.
- Geometric/precision-oriented typefaces read as forward-thinking and age well over decades — geometric clarity is not just a trend, it has a long track record.

## Patterns (name — when to use — when to avoid)
- Serif with dramatic stroke contrast — luxury/fashion/editorial positioning — avoid for anything needing to work at favicon/small mobile scale without a simplified variant.
- Geometric sans-serif — tech, startups, modern/digital-first brands — avoid when the brand needs to signal heritage/tradition instead of innovation.
- Ultra-bold rounded/slab serif — food, beverage, nostalgic/artisanal brands — avoid for minimalist or ultra-modern positioning, where it reads as inconsistent.
- All-caps condensed display — athletic, event, action-oriented brands — avoid as a body-text font or anywhere lowercase is required.

## Anti-patterns
- Choosing a font purely on trend/popularity without checking commercial licensing terms.
- Skipping the four-size legibility test before finalizing.
- Pairing a thin decorative font with a use case that requires small-size legibility (e.g. favicons, embroidery).

## Checklist (only if supported)
- Font tested at 8pt, 16pt, 36pt, and 72pt+.
- Font category matches the defined brand personality axis (traditional/modern, serious/playful).
- Commercial license confirmed (or a free alternative identified) before adoption.
- Secondary/body font selected if the primary is display-only or all-caps.
