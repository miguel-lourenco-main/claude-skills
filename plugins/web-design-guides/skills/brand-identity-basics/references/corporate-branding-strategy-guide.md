## Use when
Establishing or auditing the strategic layer beneath a visual identity — mission, positioning, voice — before any visual asset work, and defining how to measure whether the brand system is actually working.

## Rules
- A brand system has four foundational components that must work together: mission/values, visual identity (logo, color, typography, imagery style, visual tone), voice, and market positioning. Visual identity work done without the other three is decoration, not strategy.
- Test candidate "values" against reality: could any competitor claim this value, or is it genuinely distinctive to how you operate? If you violate a stated value under financial pressure, it's an aspiration, not a real value. A real value translates directly into an operational decision (e.g. "transparency" → published pricing).
- Positioning statement formula: "For [target customer], [brand] is the [category] that [unique benefit] because [reason to believe]." The "because" clause is what turns a claim into a defensible position — it must be backed by credible evidence.
- Define target audience by situation, not demographics: specific frustrations/goals beat generic descriptors like "small business owners aged 25-45."
- Differentiation sits at the intersection of what you do better than competitors, what the audience actually values, and where competitors' complaints/gaps cluster — find it by studying competitor reviews and where customer complaints repeat.
- Consistency compounds faster than perfection: a simple system applied uniformly builds recognition faster than a sophisticated one applied sporadically.
- Keep brand guidelines to one page with visual examples: logo usage rules, color codes (hex/RGB), font specs, and voice guidelines with sample phrases — a guideline document nobody reads is not a guideline.
- Measure brand health on three axes: loyalty (NPS — score >30 is strong, >50 is industry-leading; retention rate), efficiency (customer acquisition cost by channel; CAC:LTV should be 1:3 or better), and pricing power (win/loss rates at premium price points, discount frequency, whether customers question pricing).

## Patterns (name — when to use — when to avoid)
- One-page brand guideline with concrete voice examples — always, regardless of company size — no real avoid case; the failure mode is skipping it, not overusing it.
- Positioning-statement formula (For/is-the/that/because) — any time positioning feels vague or interchangeable with a competitor's — skip only once positioning is already sharp and validated externally.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating "visual identity" as the entirety of brand strategy while skipping mission/values/positioning.
- Generic demographic targeting instead of situational specificity.
- A values list that gets overridden whenever it's inconvenient (signals the values aren't real).
- Sporadic, inconsistent application of an otherwise-good visual system.

## Checklist (only if supported)
- Positioning statement written and the "because" clause backed by real evidence.
- Values tested: not competitor-claimable, and upheld under real pressure.
- One-page guideline exists covering logo, color, type, and voice with examples.
- NPS, retention, and CAC:LTV tracked, not just vibes.
