## Use when
Designing or validating a white (or any single-color/reversed) version of a mark meant to sit on dark backgrounds, photography overlays, or dark-themed products.

## Rules
- Contrast floor: maintain at least a 4.5:1 contrast ratio against the background (WCAG-derived) — pure black, navy, deep green, deep red, or any deeply saturated dark color qualifies; light backgrounds (pastels, beige, light gray) will make the mark disappear. Plan a separate colored version for light contexts from the start.
- Simplify aggressively for single-color use: limit to 1-2 primary shapes and remove any detail smaller than roughly 10% of total logo height — color differentiation between elements disappears in white, so details that were distinct in multi-color become an indistinguishable mass.
- Practical shrink test: reduce the logo to roughly one inch square; if details disappear or merge, simplify further.
- For typography in a white/single-color mark, use fonts with open counters (visible interior space in letters like O, P, B) at bold or medium weight; thin, decorative, or condensed fonts lose their internal structure and become blobs against dark backgrounds.
- Negative space is an active design element, not empty space: every white shape creates a corresponding dark shape around it, and both must be intentional. Audit by squinting or viewing at 50% opacity — if the in-between dark shapes look random, rearrange or simplify until both positive and negative forms read as deliberate.
- File format matters more for single-color marks than any other: use PNG-24 or SVG with true transparency; JPG cannot support transparency and will trap the mark inside a visible rectangle. Always confirm transparency by checking for the checkered pattern in the export preview, never trust the file extension alone.
- Export at multiple resolutions: web at 2x display size (roughly 500-1000px wide), print at 300 DPI / 12in wide; keep an SVG for infinite scaling and a vector source (AI/EPS) for professional print vendors.

## Patterns (name — when to use — when to avoid)
- White-on-dark as the sole logo variant — when the brand consistently lives on dark surfaces (dark UI, dark merch, photography overlays) and simplicity/consistency matter more than color branding — avoid if the brand also needs strong shelf/print presence on light backgrounds without a second variant ready.
- Squint/50%-opacity negative-space audit — anytime finalizing any reversed or single-color mark — no real avoid case, it's a cheap diagnostic.

## Anti-patterns
- Exporting a white logo as JPG (loses transparency, gets a visible box).
- Keeping full multi-color detail level when converting to single-color — details that read fine in color become mush in white.
- Testing only on solid black and skipping photography/textured/small-size contexts.

## Checklist (only if supported)
- Contrast ratio ≥4.5:1 verified against every intended background.
- Reduced to 1-2 shapes; no detail smaller than ~10% of logo height.
- Passes the one-inch shrink test.
- Negative space passes the squint/50%-opacity test.
- Tested on: solid darks (black, #333, navy, dark green, burgundy), 5-10 dark photographs, textured surfaces (fabric/wood/concrete), favicon size (16x16px) and large scale, transparency confirmed (no white halo) on merch mockups.
