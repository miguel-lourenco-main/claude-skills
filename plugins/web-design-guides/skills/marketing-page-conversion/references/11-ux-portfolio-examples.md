## Use when
Structuring a UX/product design portfolio meant to get interviews, deciding how many case studies to build and how deep to go on process.

## Rules
- Structure each case study as Problem → Approach → Process → Artifacts → Impact → Learnings; open with a clear problem statement, not a polished final screen.
- Show actual process (wireframes, alternative directions, decisions) rather than only finished screens — reviewers evaluate judgment, not just visual output.
- Anchor every case study's conclusion in a measurable outcome or metric.
- Limit to 2-3 excellent case studies rather than many mediocre ones; depth beats breadth.
- Be explicit about your specific role/contribution on each project (critical on team projects).
- A homepage grid of project thumbnails lets visitors assess range before committing to a deep read.

## Patterns (name — when to use — when to avoid)
- Minimal/monochrome layout with strong typography — when the work itself needs zero competition for attention — avoid if you lack 2-3 genuinely strong pieces to fill the resulting silence.
- Unconventional/nostalgic visual concept (e.g. retro OS shell) — when you want memorability over polish-competition and can execute it with craft — avoid as an ironic gimmick with no follow-through.
- Interactive AI chatbot trained on your case studies — when you can guarantee accurate answers about your own work — avoid without fine-tuning on verified content (wrong answers about your experience cost more trust than a typo).

## Anti-patterns
- Portfolio as static gallery with no articulated problem/process.
- Uniform visual treatment breaking down between projects (signals inconsistency).

## Checklist (only if supported)
- Every case study states the user problem before showing solutions.
- At least one measurable outcome per case study.
- 2-3 case studies total, not a dump of every project.
