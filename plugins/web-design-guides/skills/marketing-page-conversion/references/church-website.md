## Use when
Designing a church or congregation site that has to simultaneously serve first-time visitors, existing members, and ministry/volunteer operations.

## Rules
- Design for three distinct audiences on one site, not one generic visitor: first-time visitors need service times/location/children's-ministry info/evidence of community values; existing members need calendars, giving, and ministry resources; staff/volunteers need coordination tools. A site optimized only for visitors misses half its job.
- 80% of prospective attendees check the website before visiting in person (unsourced figure in guide) — treat the site as the actual first impression, not a brochure that happens to exist.
- The non-negotiable visitor-facing info block is: service times, directions/parking, children's-ministry details, and evidence of community values. Missing any one of these is cited as a reason visitors don't follow through.
- Online-giving transaction fees can exceed the cost of a dedicated platform at moderate volume: a church processing $10,000/month was estimated to pay $3,480/year more at a ~2.9% card rate than on a 0%-fee giving platform (guide's own illustrative calculation, unsourced third-party source) — model total giving-platform cost against monthly donation volume, not just the sticker subscription price.
- Match platform choice to actual ministry complexity, not budget alone: template builders (Wix/Squarespace-tier, ~$12-25/mo) hit walls fast on volunteer scheduling, prayer-request privacy, and member portals; WordPress gives flexibility and lower giving-transaction fees but needs either a technical volunteer or paid maintenance; dedicated church platforms (e.g., Tithe.ly, Nucleus-tier) cover standard giving/sermon-hosting needs well but strain on highly specific workflows (skill/geography-based volunteer routing, granular-permission member directories, campaign-specific donation reporting).
- A small church plant and a large established congregation have materially different feature needs (e.g., 75-member vs 500-member) — don't default to a large-church feature set for a small one or vice versa.
- .church domains signal purpose immediately but have a lower first-year price than renewal (illustrative: ~$12 first year, ~$79 at renewal in guide) — budget for the renewal jump, not just the intro price. .org still reads as standard nonprofit credibility.
- Spread website/content maintenance across multiple named volunteers by content type (sermons, events, announcements) rather than one person — this is the stated failure mode that lets church sites go stale.

## Page anatomy / must-have sections
Homepage (service times, location, welcoming message, primary CTA) → About (beliefs, history, leadership, what to expect) → Contact (address, phone, email, embedded map, multiple channels) → Online Giving (secure, recurring option, mobile-optimized, automated receipts) → Sermon library (searchable by date/speaker/series/topic) → Events calendar → Member portal (directory, giving history, RSVP) → Volunteer scheduling (availability + reminders) → Prayer-request system (with a confidential-submission option).

## Patterns (name — when to use — when to avoid)
- **Multi-audience homepage triage** — visitor-facing essentials above the fold, member/ministry tools one level down. Use for any congregation site regardless of size. Avoid a visitor-only homepage that buries giving/sermons/events for existing members.
- **Skill/team-based volunteer scheduling** — slots organized by ministry team (worship, children's, hospitality, tech, parking) with trained-role matching. Use once a congregation runs multiple concurrent Sunday ministries; unnecessary overhead for a very small congregation coordinating by hand.
- **Tiered member-directory privacy** — public/small-group/staff-only visibility levels on one underlying database. Use when the congregation has active small groups or sensitive member data; skip for very small, high-trust congregations where a simple directory suffices.

## Anti-patterns
- Choosing a platform for its low sticker subscription price without modeling per-transaction giving fees against actual donation volume.
- Applying a large-congregation feature set (complex volunteer routing, granular directory permissions) to a small church plant, or the reverse.
- Concentrating all content maintenance in one volunteer, which is the guide's stated cause of stale sermon/event content.

## Checklist
- [ ] Service times, location, and children's-ministry info are visible without navigating
- [ ] Online giving supports recurring gifts and mobile, with automated receipts
- [ ] Total giving-platform cost modeled against real monthly donation volume (subscription vs. per-transaction fee)
- [ ] Sermon library is searchable by date/speaker/series/topic
- [ ] Content-maintenance responsibility is split across named volunteers by type
- [ ] Prayer-request submissions have a confidentiality option
