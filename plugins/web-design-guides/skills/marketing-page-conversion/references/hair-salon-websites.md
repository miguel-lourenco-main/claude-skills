## Use when
Designing a local service-business site (salon and similar) whose core conversion event is a booking, especially where a meaningful share of bookings happen after hours.

## Rules
- After-hours booking capacity matters directly: 46% of bookings happen outside business hours (unsourced figure in guide) — the site itself, not a phone line, has to be able to close the sale at any hour.
- Mobile is the primary booking surface: 70% of salon appointments are booked on phones (unsourced figure in guide). Use 44×44px minimum touch targets and test the entire booking flow on a real phone before launch.
- Embed booking directly in the site rather than redirecting to a third-party platform — every redirect is a chance to lose the visitor; real-time availability shown in-page converts better than an external link-out.
- Publish pricing directly on service descriptions or a dedicated pricing page. Hidden/must-call-for pricing is a common conversion killer for this vertical — visitors leave rather than ask.
- Use real, original photography (real stylists, real space, before/after transformation shots) — stock photography actively damages credibility in a visual-outcome business; the hero image alone often decides whether a visitor stays.
- Match visual restraint/whitespace to price positioning: heavier whitespace and a tighter, more monochromatic palette read as premium/luxury; a values-led homepage (e.g., sustainability, "fewer chemicals") pre-filters for a niche clientele; an upfront new-client offer + always-on booking suits high-volume, competitive urban markets. Pick the register that matches your actual pricing and clientele — a mismatch between site tone and price point creates hesitation rather than trust.
- Never force account creation before booking — added friction at the highest-intent moment reduces completion.
- Refresh portfolio/gallery photos on a cadence (guide suggests quarterly at minimum) — stale galleries create a mismatch between expectation and the in-chair result.

## Patterns (name — when to use — when to avoid)
- **Gallery-first homepage** — let a portfolio/before-after gallery do the persuasion instead of copy. Use when the work is highly visual and portfolio quality is strong; avoid if photography is inconsistent or dated, since the gallery becomes the argument against booking.
- **Embedded real-time booking widget** — availability and scheduling live inside the site, not a link-out. Use for any service business with online scheduling; avoid only if there's no real-time calendar to embed (a static "call us" fallback is worse for after-hours conversion).
- **Stylist/provider profile pages** — photo, short bio, specialties, direct booking link per provider. Use when clients typically have a preferred provider (common in salons); skip if the service is provider-agnostic.
- **Values-led homepage** — lead with a point of view (sustainability, ingredient philosophy) before service details. Use in crowded markets where services/pricing overlap and you want self-selecting clients; avoid if your differentiation is actually price or convenience, not values.
- **Whitespace-as-luxury-signal** — generous negative space, restrained palette. Use for higher-price-point positioning; avoid for budget/high-volume positioning where it can read as empty rather than premium.

## Page anatomy / must-have sections
Homepage (hero photo/gallery + primary booking CTA) → Services/Pricing (visible price list or ranges) → Stylist/provider profiles with booking links → Social proof (reviews, press, before/after gallery) placed near the booking CTA → Embedded booking flow reachable in 1-2 taps from any page.

## Anti-patterns
- Routing booking through an external platform with no in-page availability.
- Hiding or omitting pricing.
- Using stock photography for a visually-driven service business.
- Requiring account creation before a booking can be started.
- Letting the design register (luxury minimalism vs. discount-offer urgency) contradict the actual price point.

## Checklist
- [ ] Booking is embedded on-site with real-time availability, not a redirect
- [ ] Pricing is visible on service pages or a dedicated pricing page
- [ ] Hero image is original photography, not stock
- [ ] Booking flow tested end-to-end on an actual phone
- [ ] Stylist/provider profiles link directly to booking
- [ ] Social proof sits near the booking CTA
- [ ] Portfolio photos are refreshed at least quarterly
