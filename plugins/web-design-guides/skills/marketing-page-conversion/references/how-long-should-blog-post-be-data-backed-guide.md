## Use when
Deciding target word count for on-page content (blog posts, guides, pillar pages) as part of a marketing site's SEO/content strategy — tangential to page layout/conversion design but directly relevant to copy/SEO decisions on pages.

## Rules
- Word count itself is not a ranking factor (Google has stated there is no target word count, per guide) — the real lever is whether the content fully satisfies the searcher's intent; length is a byproduct of thoroughness, not a goal.
- Longer content correlates with more backlinks (content over 3,000 words got 77.2% more backlinks than content under 1,000 words, per a Backlinko-BuzzSumo analysis of 912M posts, per guide) — this is a correlation via backlinks, not a direct length→ranking effect; don't pad content to chase this.
- Reference baseline for competitive informational content: ~1,400–1,500 words for average first-page results (per Backlinko's 11.8M-result analysis and Orbit Media's Annual Blogger Survey, both per guide, converging independently) — but top-performing content in another dataset averaged only 1,152 words vs. 668 for low performers (per Semrush, per guide), i.e. efficiency/quality tracked with performance more than raw length.
- Most reliable sizing method: pull the top 5–10 ranking competitors for the target keyword, average their word counts, and target within ±20% of that average — rather than using a fixed rule of thumb.
- Length by content type (synthesized ranges from the guide, several sources per guide): pillar/ultimate guides 3,000–5,000+ words; how-to guides 1,700–2,500; standard blog posts 1,400–1,500; listicles 1,500–2,500 (~150–250 words/item); opinion/thought-leadership 1,000–1,500; news/updates 400–800; quick-answer/definition content 300–500.
- Go shorter than competitors when they've padded with fluff you can cut without losing coverage; go longer only when you add genuinely new value (original research, edge cases, deeper examples) — length increases without new value don't help.
- For featured-snippet targeting, write a concise, structurally isolated direct-answer passage (a tight paragraph or short list) near the top of the content — snippets favor extractable short answers regardless of overall post length.
- For AI-overview/AI-citation visibility, shorter, more concise pages were disproportionately cited: cited pages averaged 1,282 words (vs. 1,447 first-page average) and 53.4% of citations went to pages under 1,000 words, with essentially no correlation (0.04) between word count and citation position (per Ahrefs' analysis of 174,000 AI-Overview-cited pages, per guide) — do not assume a long, comprehensive page is the best target for AI-overview citation; a tight, well-structured shorter page may perform better there.

## Anti-patterns
- Padding content to hit a target word count instead of stopping once the topic is fully covered.
- Writing long-form content when targeting a quick-answer or AI-overview-style query, where concise pages outperform.
- Ignoring what's actually ranking for the target keyword and using a generic length rule instead.

## Checklist
- [ ] Checked top 5–10 ranking competitors' average length for the target keyword before drafting
- [ ] Length matches content type/intent (quick-answer vs. how-to vs. pillar) from the ranges above
- [ ] Any length beyond the competitive average adds genuinely new value, not padding
- [ ] If targeting a featured snippet or AI Overview, included a short, structurally isolated direct-answer passage
