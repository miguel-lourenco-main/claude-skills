## Use when
Building a portfolio meant to convert reviewers into interviews/inquiries (marketing, but the bio/case-study/CTA structure generalizes to any client-facing portfolio).

## Rules
- Bio formula: specific outcome delivered + for whom + through what method, under 150 words, with the strongest credential (revenue number, recognizable client, concrete metric) in the first two sentences — reviewers scan, they don't read linearly.
- Case study structure that converts: Context → Problem (with measurable stakes) → Solution/rationale → Implementation (timeline + your specific contribution) → Results (quantified). For fast scanners, lead with the outcome as the headline instead ("results-first" variant).
- Project selection: 7-10 strong recent samples overall, but 3-5 fully detailed case studies outperform 10+ shallow ones. Prioritize recency (last ~2 years), relevance to target roles, and channel/industry variety.
- Benefit-led CTAs ("Get a Strategy Review") outperform generic "Contact Me"; place them right after a demonstrated result.
- Trust signals (client logos, outcome-specific testimonials, quantified metrics) should be threaded throughout, not confined to one section. A testimonial naming a specific outcome ("increased qualified leads by 67% in three months") outperforms generic praise.
- For work without real client metrics, project realistic numbers from industry benchmarks and label them explicitly as projected — this preserves credibility while still demonstrating analytical thinking.
- Document results as you go: capture baseline "before" metrics before starting, keep a running timeline of what was implemented when, so specific tactics can be attributed to specific results.
- Update on a quarterly cadence; replace work older than ~2 years or that no longer reflects current skill/target.

## Patterns (name — when to use — when to avoid)
- Results-first case study lead — when your audience skims in seconds and the outcome is your strongest asset — avoid when the process/reasoning is what the target role actually screens for (e.g. research-heavy roles).
- Spec/mock campaign for a real brand — when you lack paid client work — avoid presenting projected numbers as if they were real, measured results (always label as projected).

## Anti-patterns
- Listing responsibilities instead of outcomes ("Marketing Manager with 5 years experience").
- Fabricating testimonials or unlabeled projected metrics.
- One universal portfolio never revisited as target roles/skills shift.

## Checklist (only if supported)
- Bio under 150 words, strongest credential in first two sentences.
- 3-5 full case studies following Context-Problem-Solution-Implementation-Results.
- Every case study ends with a quantified result or clearly labeled projection.
- CTA is benefit-specific and placed after a results statement.
