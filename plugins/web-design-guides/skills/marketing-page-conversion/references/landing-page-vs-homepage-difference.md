## Use when
Deciding whether a given piece of traffic/goal should route to a homepage or a dedicated landing page, or how to structure each.

## Rules
- Match page type to traffic intent, not to convenience: paid ads, product launches, single-offer campaigns, and email campaigns → dedicated landing page; organic brand search, direct visits, broad discovery → homepage.
- Never A/B test on a homepage — too many concurrent variables to isolate a signal. Use a landing page for any message/offer test.
- Homepage must serve multiple visitor types simultaneously: new visitor (value prop → features → CTA), returning customer (fast path to login), researcher (resources/case studies) — design multiple parallel paths, not one funnel.
- Landing page must remove standard navigation (no menu, no footer links, often an unlinked logo) and keep only sticky CTA(s); removing navigation doubled conversion (3%→6%) in a cited VWO/Yuppiechef case study (per VWO case study, per guide).
- Landing page CTA copy: first-person phrasing ("Start my free trial") converted 90% better than second-person ("Start your free trial") (per ContentVerve, per guide).
- Measure homepages on engagement (bounce rate, time on page, pages/session); measure landing pages on conversion rate / cost-per-conversion. Applying homepage-style engagement metrics to a landing page is a false read — a landing page with high time-on-page and high bounce is failing, not succeeding.
- Homepage essential elements: value proposition answerable in seconds, one primary CTA toward the top business goal, intuitive nav, quality visuals reinforcing brand, trust signals (testimonials/logos), brief product/service showcase, accessible contact info (per HubSpot, per guide).
- Homepage core functions to design against: easy access, clear purpose, engaging content, action/navigation facilitation, simplicity (per Nielsen Norman Group's 5 principles, per guide).
- Landing pages convert at a higher median rate than homepages for the same paid traffic (median landing page ~6.6% per Unbounce's 2024 Conversion Benchmark Report, per guide, vs. homepage ~2-3% cited unsourced in this guide) — this gap is the business case for building a dedicated page rather than sending paid traffic to the homepage.
- Growing businesses typically need both: homepage as brand anchor, landing pages layered on top for each campaign/segment.

## Patterns (name — when to use — when to avoid)
- Navigation-free landing page — use for any single-offer paid/email campaign page — avoid on homepage or any page meant to support open-ended exploration.
- Dual-path homepage (new vs. returning vs. researcher) — use on the primary homepage — avoid replicating this branching on a landing page (defeats the single-goal focus).

## Anti-patterns
- Sending paid traffic to the homepage instead of a matched landing page.
- Running message/offer A/B tests on the homepage.
- Judging a landing page's success by homepage-style engagement metrics.

## Checklist
- [ ] Paid/email/launch traffic has a dedicated landing page, not the homepage
- [ ] Landing page has no standard nav, single CTA repeated at strategic points
- [ ] CTA copy uses first-person phrasing
- [ ] Homepage vs. landing page success is measured on the correct metric set (engagement vs. conversion)
