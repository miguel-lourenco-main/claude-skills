## Use when
Designing or auditing a SaaS marketing site (homepage, pricing, feature pages, nav) — deciding what to build first by company stage.

## Rules
- Lead every headline/hero with the visitor's problem, not the product's features — most homepage visitors are only problem-aware, not product-aware (per CXL research on awareness stages, per guide). Introduce features only after problem and solution framing (per Copyhackers, per guide).
- Write at a 5th–7th grade reading level: SaaS pages at that level converted at 12.9% vs. 2.1% for complex/professional copy (per Unbounce, per guide) — treat copy simplicity as a top-tier lever, not polish.
- Show the product (interactive preview, sandbox, or opt-in free trial) before any signup wall; every added barrier extends time-to-value and reduces conversion. Opt-in trials (no card) generate more trial starts; opt-out trials (card required) generate a higher near-term paid-conversion percentage but risk trust/retention (per CXL, per guide) — pick deliberately based on GTM motion, not default.
- Treat peer/social proof as structural, not a separate "testimonials" section: place logos, case stats, and review counts adjacent to CTAs, below pricing tiers, and on feature pages. A testimonial-heading placement change alone lifted trial sign-ups 22% (per VWO/Buildium case study, per guide).
- Publish pricing rather than gating it behind "Contact Us": a pricing-page visit is a strong buyer-intent signal your sales team loses if pricing is hidden (per OpenView Partners, per guide). Most private SaaS unicorns publish pricing (55%) vs. public SaaS companies (28%) (per OpenView, per guide).
- Design the first viewport (hero) to communicate what/who/what's-next within ~10 seconds — users decide whether to keep reading in that window (per NNG research, per guide).
- Above-the-fold content gets disproportionately more eye-tracking attention than just-below-fold content, but the single most-viewed strip sits ~550px down the page because users scroll before the page finishes loading (per NNG eye-tracking data and a Chartbeat session study, per guide, cited separately) — design the hero to read well mid-scroll, not only from a static top-down read.
- Put activation CTAs (trial/demo/pricing) directly in primary navigation, not just the hero — this out-produces nav built purely for information architecture (per CXL/ConvertKit case, per guide, demo requests tripled on mobile after this change).
- Pair a low-friction CTA with objection-defusing microcopy directly adjacent ("Start free" + "No credit card required" + "Full access") to address cost/commitment/feature-access objections simultaneously.
- Build distinct conversion logic per page type (homepage vs. feature page vs. blog post vs. pricing page) — most visitors don't land on the homepage. Personalized CTAs convert far better than generic ones (per HubSpot, per guide, +202%).
- Put trust signals everywhere a commitment is asked, using two layers: credibility-layer (logos, named customers, usage stats) early on each page to establish legitimacy; submission-layer (SSL badges, privacy statements) right next to form fields/CTAs where submission anxiety spikes (per Baymard, per guide).
- Match CTA friction level to your actual go-to-market motion: low-friction (email-only, instant access) signals self-serve; high-friction (credit card, multi-field form, sales call) signals enterprise. The mismatch — not the friction level itself — is what costs conversions.
- Treat the site as never "finished": run continuous, sequential A/B tests rather than periodic full redesigns; sustained iteration beat single-redesign approaches in cited case data (per Optimizely research, per guide).

## Page anatomy (priority order by company stage)
- Pre-launch: problem-first messaging, above-the-fold clarity, CTA-friction match to GTM motion.
- Early stage (first ~100 customers): product preview before signup wall, structural social proof placement, transparent pricing.
- Scaling: activation-oriented navigation, mid-funnel page-specific conversion logic, trust-signal coverage across all pages, sustained iteration/testing cadence.

## Anti-patterns
- Feature-first hero copy aimed at a problem-aware (not product-aware) visitor.
- Gating pricing behind a "Contact Us" form by default.
- Treating social proof as a single homepage section instead of distributing it near every commitment point.
- One conversion-logic template applied to homepage, feature pages, and blog posts alike.
- CTA friction level that doesn't match the actual sales motion (e.g., demanding a sales call for a self-serve product).

## Checklist
- [ ] Hero leads with the visitor's problem before naming the product/features
- [ ] Product is visible/tryable before any signup wall
- [ ] Pricing is public and scannable
- [ ] Trust signals appear in two layers (credibility early, submission-anxiety-adjacent near forms)
- [ ] Primary nav includes a trial/demo/pricing CTA
- [ ] CTA friction level matches the GTM motion (self-serve vs. enterprise)
- [ ] Different page types (home/feature/blog/pricing) have distinct, tailored CTAs
