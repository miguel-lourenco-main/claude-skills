## Strengths
- Ruthlessly committed theme: every choice (neon-outline logo, pixel nav font, Space Invader icon, scattered pixel stars, 'Ta mission est claire' copy) reinforces retrogaming — no generic restaurant template leaking through
- Two-novelty-display-font system is risky but executed well: NeoNeon for emotion/headlines, Invasion2000 for arcade flavor, Ubuntu carrying readability — clear roles, not random
- Disciplined 4-color palette (navy / steel-blue / cyan / yellow) gives high contrast and a recognizable brand even though the type is wild; yellow is reserved strictly for the one word that matters per headline
- Diagonal divider + skewed images (objectfit-skew) create energy and motion in an otherwise grid layout, echoing CRT/glitch aesthetics without being gimmicky
- Strong, specific, voice-driven French microcopy (tutoiement, Kaméhaméha/Worms/Duck Hunt references) — copy does as much branding work as the visuals
- Genuinely useful interactivity: live AJAX 'is your favorite game here?' search and the tabbed 5-spaces explorer turn a static one-pager into something worth exploring

## Weaknesses
- Two ornate display fonts plus animated outline logo flirts with legibility/clutter at small sizes; the NeoNeon outline headline can get hard to read over busy areas
- Heavy WordPress/plugin stack (Yoast, WP Rocket, jQuery, Locomotive smooth-scroll, SearchWP) is a lot of weight for a single page and the smooth-scroll can feel sluggish/finicky on lower-end devices
- Per-letter split-text animation (every character wrapped in its own transformed div) bloats the DOM and is an accessibility/SEO smell for the H1

## Notable patterns
- Reserve one accent color for emphasis-per-headline: highlight exactly one word in arcade-yellow (e.g. '5 espaces', 'Soif ?', 'sur Instagram !') so each section title has a single focal point against an otherwise white-on-navy scale
- Animated SVG outline logo as the hero centerpiece: a single-stroke neon 'draw-on' wordmark/mascot doubles as both branding and the main hero animation, removing the need for a separate hero graphic
- Diagonal white divider + skewed (clip/transform) imagery to inject CRT-era energy into a standard two-column hero without leaving the grid
- Tabbed in-place content swapper ('1 bar, 5 espaces'): a vertical list of clickable H3 labels that swaps the adjacent copy+image, packing five 'rooms' into one viewport-height section
- On-brand utility widget: a live AJAX search styled as the theme ('Cherche un jeu !' with a Space Invader icon) turns a feature into a brand moment instead of a plain form

## When to use
Use this approach for a strongly themed, single-location concept bar or experiential venue (retro/arcade, board-game cafe, geek/anime bar) where a bold sub-cultural identity and playful voice matter more than menu-ordering utility.
